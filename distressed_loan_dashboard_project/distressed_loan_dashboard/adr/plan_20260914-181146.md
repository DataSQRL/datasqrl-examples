## Requirements Summary

Build a greenfield DataSQRL **batch** pipeline, `distress_dashboard`, that each night recomputes weekly loan-payment-distress aggregates over a rolling 13-complete-week window and serves them through a GraphQL / REST / MCP API, plus a single-page dashboard UI over that API.

In DataSQRL terms:

- **Sources** are the shared `data-catalog` module, included under namespace `data_catalog`: consumer-loan, credit-card and mortgage payment tables; the three matching delinquency-event tables; the four loan/account tables that carry the primary borrower; and customer master + customer address for geography and display fields. Production reads the catalog's Kafka connectors; test and local read project-owned filesystem test data declared in `connectors/sources-test.sqrl` with `LIKE` against the catalog's `*_schema` tables.
- **Processing** runs in Flink `BATCH` mode. Every posted payment and every unpaid `MISSED_PAYMENT` delinquency event is normalized into one distress "slot", classified into exactly one of *posted on time* / *posted late* / *not posted*, attributed to the instrument's primary borrower and that borrower's current primary-address zip code, bucketed by Monday-start week, aggregated per (customer, week), then rolled up per (zip, week).
- **Serving** is Postgres behind Vert.x: `DashboardRun`, `ZipWeekly`, `ZipOverview`, `CustomerWeekly`, plus the table functions `ZipOverviewByPrefix`, `ZipWeeklyHistory` and `ZipCustomerDistress`. Engines are `flink`, `postgres`, `vertx` — no Kafka (no mutations, no subscriptions) and no Iceberg.
- **Monitoring** adds nine `DQ_*` capture tables and two `OB_*` statistics tables, enumerated in `## Data Quality & Observability`.

**UI is required.** R7–R14 describe a dashboard with a filterable/sortable zip overview table, a click-through drill-down, a 12-week chart and a sortable customer table. Deferring "visual/UI design details" (Section 6 of the requirements) does not make this deliverable API-only. The plan therefore carries dedicated UI checklist steps (steps 20–22) covering: the zip overview table with search and client-side sorting, the drill-down panel (chart + two-week distressed summary + customer table), the GraphQL queries those views issue, the staleness banner fed by `DashboardRun`, validation against a locally running pipeline, and a `ui/README.md` plus a pointer from the project `README.md`.

### Business definitions (Section 4 of the requirements, verbatim)

**Weekly payment buckets (by week due).** Each payment due in a week falls into exactly one of the following buckets for that week:

- **Posted on time:** A payment due in the week that was posted by its due date.
- **Posted late:** A payment due in the week that was posted after its due date. This includes payments that were recorded as delinquency events and paid later.
- **Not posted:** A payment due in the week that has not been posted and is recorded as a delinquency event.

No grace periods apply: any payment posted after its due date is late.

Derived from the buckets:

- **Payments due:** All payments due in the week (posted on time + posted late + not posted).
- **Distressed:** Posted late + not posted.
- **Percent distressed:** Distressed amount divided by payments due amount, for the same week. Always between 0% and 100%, so it can be compared week over week.

Each bucket is measured by both count and amount. Percent distressed uses amounts.

A payment is never counted in both posted late and not posted. When a delinquent payment is eventually paid, it moves from not posted to posted late.

The distressed total for a week is stable: a payment that enters the distressed bucket stays there, even if it is eventually paid.

**Lateness (by week posted).**

- **Average days late:** The average days late of all late payments posted during the week, regardless of when they were due. This includes payments that were due before the reporting window.

Average days late is a separate metric from the buckets. It is grouped by the week a payment was *posted*, while the buckets are grouped by the week a payment was *due*, so the two are not comparable. It only reflects payments that were eventually posted. Payments that remain unpaid appear in the not posted bucket and do not affect average days late.

**Other.**

- **Payment amounts:** Taken as provided in the input data for all loan vehicles, including credit cards.
- **Customer:** For loans with more than one borrower, the primary borrower.
- **Geography:** The customer's zip code as it stands when the numbers are computed. This applies to all history in the reporting window, for every loan vehicle. Each zip code's state is used only to filter zip codes. There are no state-level totals.
- **Week:** The reporting period for all aggregations.

**Assumptions (to be confirmed) — stated by the requirements.**

- A delinquency event is created in the week the missed payment was due. This is what makes the distressed total available and stable right after the week ends.
- Partial payments are ignored for now.

### Functional requirements

- **R1** — For each week and each zip code, provide the count and amount of payments due, posted on time, posted late, not posted, and distressed, plus percent distressed and average days late. Each zip code is associated with its state for filtering.
- **R2** — Provide the same measures for each week and each customer.
- **R3** — The aggregations are computed by a nightly batch job.
- **R4** — Each night, the job recomputes all aggregations over the last three months, replacing the previous results. For the buckets, this covers payments due in that period. For average days late, it covers late payments posted in that period, whenever they were due.
- **R5** — Because zip codes are assigned at computation time, a customer who moves is attributed to their new zip code for the entire window on the next nightly run. This is expected behavior.
- **R6** — The split between posted late and not posted for a past week changes on later runs as delinquent payments are paid. The distressed total does not. This is expected behavior.
- **R7** — Display a table with one row per zip code, showing its state and the amounts for payments due, posted late, not posted, and distressed, plus percent distressed and average days late.
- **R8** — The table shows the last two weeks and the trend between them: whether each measure increased or decreased, and by how much.
  - For amounts and average days late, the trend is the percentage change (e.g., $10,000 to $12,000 is +20%).
  - For percent distressed, the trend is the difference between the two percentages (e.g., 5% to 6% is +1%; 9% to 6% is −3%).
- **R9** — Provide a text search field above the table:
  - Numeric input shows all zip codes starting with that prefix (e.g., "981").
  - Alphabetic input shows all zip codes in that state (e.g., "PA").
  - Empty input shows all zip codes.
- **R10** — The table shows only zip codes matching the current filter. It can be sorted by any column, ascending or descending, by clicking the column header.
- **R11** — Clicking a zip code in the overview table displays the following below it:
  - A 12-week chart for that zip code (see R12).
  - The zip code's distressed count and distressed amount for the last two weeks, each with its week-over-week percentage change.
  - A customer table limited to customers in that zip code with at least one distressed payment (posted late or not posted).
- **R12** — The chart plots the following for each of the last 12 weeks: Amount of payments posted on time; Amount of payments distressed; Percent distressed; Average days late. The first three are by week due. Average days late is by week posted.
- **R13** — The customer table shows, for each customer, the customer ID and enough high-level customer information for the user to look the customer up in other systems, plus all measures from R2.
- **R14** — The customer table can be sorted by any column, ascending or descending, by clicking the column header, so the most distressed customers can be brought to the top.
- **R15** — The dashboard is served by a GraphQL API. All data the dashboard displays comes through this API.
- **R16** — The API supports everything the dashboard does: filtering zip codes by prefix or state, the two-week overview with trends, a zip code's 12-week history, and the customer table with sorting. It also provides all measures from R1 and R2, including those not shown on the dashboard (such as zip-level counts for each bucket).
- **R17** — The same API operations are exposed through MCP, so an AI agent can use them to automate parts of this workflow in the future.

### Out of scope (Section 6 of the requirements, verbatim)

- State-level totals
- Grace periods
- Partial payments
- Special handling of loans in hardship arrangements (forbearance, deferral, modification)
- Loan- and payment-level detail for individual customers. Users look this up in other systems.
- Schemas, data models, and source systems
- Visual/UI design details
- Intervention workflows (outreach, case management). The dashboard supports *planning* interventions; executing them is assumed out of scope. Please confirm.

### Open questions (Section 7 of the requirements, verbatim)

Needs business input:

1. **Customer table time window:** Should it show the last two weeks with trend (like the overview), all 12 weeks, or a single selected week? This also determines the period in which a customer must have a distressed payment to appear.
2. **Loan vehicle:** Should users be able to break out or filter by loan vehicle in either table or the chart?
3. **Access and privacy:** Which employees, and which agents through MCP, may see customer-level data? Are masking or audit logging required?

Deferred to the technical and design teams:

4. **Week definition:** Week start day, time zone, and using the most recent *complete* weeks for the "last two weeks" and the chart.
5. **Window vs. chart:** Three months is roughly 13 weeks. Ensure the window always covers 12 complete weeks.

7. **Customer information fields:** Which high-level fields to show alongside the customer ID.
8. **Chart layout:** Amounts, a percentage, and days on one chart with separate axes, or several aligned charts. Since average days late is grouped by a different week than the other measures, the layout should not suggest they are directly comparable.
9. **Default sort order** for the overview and customer tables.
10. **State input format:** Two-letter abbreviations, full names, or both, and handling of mixed alphanumeric input.
11. **API and MCP design:** How API operations map to MCP tools, and how agents authenticate.
12. **Scale:** Number of customers and payments, and expected concurrent users.
13. **Job failure:** Showing the previous night's results if a run fails, and showing when data was last refreshed.

Every one of these is resolved with a concrete default in `## Assumptions`. None is left open.

Every source is a **CDC stream from the source system** (stated in each catalog dataset header). Delivery is at-least-once; the same logical row may arrive more than once with a newer `source_updated_at`. Every source table therefore carries:

- `source_system STRING NOT NULL` and `source_updated_at TIMESTAMP_LTZ(3) NOT NULL` as payload columns, and
- `ingested_at TIMESTAMP_LTZ(3)` added by the connector table (Kafka record timestamp in prod, a physical field in every test record), watermarked `ingested_at - INTERVAL '30' SECOND` in prod and `- INTERVAL '10' SECOND` in test.

**Deduplication key and tiebreaker**: each table's own primary identifier (`payment_id`, `delinquency_event_id`, `loan_id`, `credit_card_account_id`, `customer_id`, or (`customer_id`, `address_type`, `effective_from`)), keeping the row with the greatest `source_updated_at`. No ordering guarantee across tables.

**Expected volume** (assumed, see `## Assumptions`): ≤ 5 million payment rows and ≤ 500 thousand customers inside a 13-week window.

**Constraint from out-of-scope features**: hardship arrangements (forbearance, deferral, modification) are out of scope, but `Loan_Modification` / `loss_mitigation_status` data exists in the catalog and must remain reachable — do not filter loans by modification status anywhere in the pipeline, so the exclusion can be added later without re-plumbing the payment path.

The mortgage table's `LATE_PAYMENT` value is especially dangerous: it describes a payment that *was* eventually made and is already represented in `Mortgage_Payment`.

### Contract deviations to record in the README

1. **Sorting (R10, R14) is client-side.** GraphQL/SQRL does not generate dynamic `sort_by` / `sort_dir` arguments, and the result sets are small (zip codes matching one filter; customers of one zip code). Every operation returns a deterministic default order and the UI sorts the full returned set in the browser. `compiler.api.default-limit` is raised to `1000` so the full filtered set is returned in one call.
2. **The zip search field (R9) is disambiguated by the client.** The API exposes two operations — `ZipOverview` with an optional `state` equality filter and `ZipOverviewByPrefix` with a required prefix — and the UI chooses between them based on whether the first character of the input is a digit. This is a deliberate split: an MCP agent gets two unambiguous tools instead of one overloaded string argument.

## Scenario Analysis

### Axis 1 — artifact type: `pipeline`

The deliverable ingests from external sources, aggregates, and exposes an API. Its files are `.sqrl` scripts plus `*-package.json` configuration, and it consumes an existing catalog rather than defining one. It declares no `_schema` tables, no ontology, and no per-environment connector files for shared datasets — those all live in the read-only `data-catalog` sibling and are reused through `script.include`. R15–R17 make a served GraphQL/REST/MCP API a first-class requirement, which a catalog scenario never carries. Therefore `scenario: pipeline`, not `catalog`.

### Axis 2 — task type: `new-project`

The project directory contains no `.sqrl` file, no `*-package.json`, no `README.md` and no connectors — only the requirements ADR and the skills directory. There is nothing to extend, so it is not `extend-project`. It is not `connector-setup`: connectors are a small part of the work and the bulk is aggregation logic and API design. It is not `architecture-design` either: the architecture is determinable now (batch Flink → Postgres → Vert.x over an existing catalog, with the closest template already identified), and this plan commits to it rather than deferring, so the next phase writes code rather than more design. Therefore `new-project`.

### Architecture determined by DataSQRL constraints

- **Batch, not streaming.** R3 and R4 specify a nightly job that recomputes the whole window and replaces prior results, and R5 requires a customer's *entire* window history to be re-attributed to a new zip code on the next run. Neither is expressible as an incremental streaming aggregate — a zip change would have to retract every past week. Flink `execution.runtime-mode: BATCH` is the construct that matches.
- **No Kafka engine.** There are no mutations and no `SUBSCRIBE` statements, so the `kafka` engine is omitted. Production *sources* are Kafka topics read through the catalog's connectors, which does not require the Kafka engine.
- **No Iceberg.** There is no data-lake output; the only sink is the served Postgres database.
- **A run anchor is mandatory.** "Last three months" must be pinned to a deterministic date in test or every snapshot rots. The anchor is one templated expression resolving to `CURRENT_DATE` in prod (evaluated once at query start in batch mode) and to a fixed `DATE` literal in test and local.
- **The catalog's `{{is_batch}}` must be supplied.** The catalog's connector scripts branch on `is_batch` and the catalog's own package config does not define it, so this project's `script.include` entry must pass it as `true`.
- **Project-owned test data.** The catalog's test data holds 7 consumer-loan payments, 5 credit-card payments and one delinquency event per lending dataset, all dated 2023–2024. It cannot exercise a 13-week window, five zip codes, three states, four instrument types, or any of the acceptance oracles in `## Acceptance Criteria`. This project therefore declares its own filesystem connectors with `LIKE` against the catalog's `*_schema` tables and owns its test data. The catalog is still the single source of schema truth; nothing is copied.

## Acceptance Criteria

Every criterion below is copied from Section 8 of the requirements and bound to the requirement it verifies and, where the test data supplies the oracle, to the record identifiers from `## Test Data Specification`.

### Buckets and measures

- *(R4, Section 4 buckets; T4)* A payment posted on or before its due date counts only in posted on time.
- *(R4, Section 4 buckets; T5)* A payment posted one day after its due date counts as posted late.
- *(R4, Section 4 buckets; T6, T12)* A payment due in week 1 that is not made appears in week 1's not posted bucket.
- *(R6; T13)* If that payment is paid in week 4, it moves to week 1's posted late bucket, is removed from not posted, and week 1's distressed count and amount do not change. Concretely: zip 30301's W-8 shows `posted_late_amount_cents` 250000, `not_posted_count` 0 and `distressed_amount_cents` 250000.
- *(Section 4 lateness; T13)* That payment's days late count toward week 4's average days late, because it was posted in week 4. Concretely: 30301's W-5 `avg_days_late` includes the value 20.
- *(R4, Section 4 lateness; T14)* A payment due five months ago and paid this week, 150 days late, counts toward this week's average days late. It does not appear in any bucket, because it was due before the window. Concretely: 98109's W0 `avg_days_late` = 150.00 and no bucket anywhere contains 180000.
- *(R1; T7)* If a zip code has $100,000 in payments due for a week, of which $3,000 were posted late and $2,000 were not posted, percent distressed shows 5%. Concretely: zip 98101, W-1, `payments_due_amount_cents` 10000000, `posted_late_amount_cents` 300000, `not_posted_amount_cents` 200000, `distressed_amount_cents` 500000, `pct_distressed` 5.00.
- *(R1)* Percent distressed is always between 0% and 100%. Verified by the no-rows invariant test in checklist step 18.
- *(R1)* For each week, posted on time plus distressed equals payments due, by both count and amount. Verified by the no-rows invariant test in checklist step 18.
- *(R1, R2)* For a given week, a zip code's bucket counts and amounts equal the sum of the figures of all its customers (R2), including customers not shown in the drill-down. Verified by the no-rows invariant test in checklist step 18, and by construction because `ZipWeekly` is a rollup of `CustomerWeekly`.
- *(Section 4 "Customer"; T27)* A loan with two borrowers is counted once, under the primary borrower and the primary borrower's zip code.

### Processing

- *(R3, R4)* A payment posted since the previous nightly run is reflected after the next run.
- *(R5; T1)* After a customer's zip code changes, the next nightly run attributes all of that customer's payments in the window to the new zip code. Concretely: `CUST-105`'s entire window is attributed to 19104 and nothing appears under 19125.

### Dashboard

- *(R8; T8, T9)* If a zip code's distressed amount went from $10,000 to $12,000 between the last two weeks, the overview shows +20%. Concretely: zip 19104, `distressed_amount_cents` 1000000 then 1200000, `distressed_amount_pct_change` 20.00.
- *(R8; T8, T9, T10, T11)* If a zip code's percent distressed went from 5% to 6%, the overview shows +1%. If it went from 9% to 6%, it shows −3%. Concretely: zip 19104 `pct_distressed_point_change` 1.00; zip 19147 `pct_distressed_point_change` −3.00.
- *(R9)* Entering "981" shows only zip codes beginning with 981. Concretely: `ZipOverviewByPrefix` with `zip_prefix` `981` returns exactly 98101 and 98109.
- *(R9)* Entering "PA" shows all zip codes in Pennsylvania, and no state total row. Concretely: `ZipOverview` with `state` `PA` returns exactly 19104, 19147 and 15213, and no aggregate row.
- *(R10)* Clicking any column header in the overview table sorts by that column; clicking it again reverses the order.
- *(R11, R12)* Clicking a zip code shows a 12-week chart, the zip code's distressed count and amount with week-over-week change, and a table of that zip code's customers with at least one distressed payment, each with a customer ID and all measures. Concretely: `ZipWeeklyHistory` for 98101 returns exactly twelve rows, the earliest beginning 2026-06-22 (T29).
- *(R11; T28)* A customer in the zip code with no distressed payments does not appear in the customer table. Concretely: `ZipCustomerDistress` for 15213 returns no rows.
- *(R14)* Clicking any column header in the customer table sorts by that column; clicking it again reverses the order.

### API and agent access

- *(R15)* Every figure shown on the dashboard can be retrieved through the GraphQL API.
- *(R16)* Zip-level counts for every bucket are available through the API, even though the dashboard shows only the distressed count. Concretely: `ZipWeekly` and `ZipOverview` expose `payments_due_count`, `posted_on_time_count`, `posted_late_count`, `not_posted_count` and `distressed_count`.
- *(R17)* The same queries made through MCP return the same results as through the GraphQL API.

## Data Quality & Observability

### Build

- **`DQ_Payment_Unknown_Instrument`** — data quality capture. Reads the left join from the normalized payment union to the instrument-owner union. One row is a posted payment whose loan or credit-card account record does not exist, so it cannot be attributed to a customer or a zip code and is silently absent from every bucket. Carries the payment identifier, instrument type, instrument identifier, amount, due date, posted date and ingestion timestamp. Used by the lending data team to reconcile payment feeds against loan master feeds. Supports the R1/R2 sum identity, which a dropped payment would break invisibly. Exercised by T17. Filterable by instrument type.
- **`DQ_Delinquency_Unknown_Instrument`** — data quality capture. Reads the left join from the missed-payment delinquency union to the instrument-owner union. One row is a missed-payment event whose loan or account record does not exist, so the not-posted amount it represents is missing from its week entirely. Carries the event identifier, instrument type, instrument identifier, the event's own customer identifier, amount past due and event date. Same audience and purpose as the row above. Exercised by T18. Filterable by instrument type.
- **`DQ_Payment_Missing_Dates`** — data quality capture. Reads the classified payment table. One row is a payment that the source marked applied or posted but that cannot be bucketed, with an `issue` column distinguishing a missing due date from a missing posted date. A missing due date makes the payment unbucketable by week due; a missing posted date makes on-time versus late undecidable. Carries the payment identifier, instrument type and identifier, amount, due date, posted date and the issue. Used by the servicing operations team. Exercised by T19 and T20. Filterable by instrument type and issue.
- **`DQ_Payment_DaysLate_Mismatch`** — data quality capture. Reads the classified payment table. One row is a posted payment whose derived days late, computed as posted date minus due date, disagrees with the lateness the source reported in `days_past_due_at_payment` or `days_from_due`. This is the runtime guard on the requirements' "no grace periods apply" rule: a systematic mismatch means the source is applying a grace period the dashboard must not inherit. Carries the payment identifier, instrument type and identifier, due date, posted date, derived days late and source days late. Used by the credit risk team. Exercised by T21. Filterable by instrument type.
- **`DQ_Delinquency_Missing_Amount`** — data quality capture. Reads the missed-payment delinquency union. One row is a missed-payment event whose amount past due is null or zero, which is counted in the not-posted count but contributes nothing to the not-posted amount, so the week's percent distressed is understated. Carries the event identifier, instrument type and identifier, customer identifier and event date. Used by the collections data team. Exercised by T22. Filterable by instrument type.
- **`DQ_Delinquency_Customer_Mismatch`** — data quality capture. Reads the join of the missed-payment delinquency union to the instrument-owner union. One row is an event whose own customer identifier differs from the instrument's primary borrower. The pipeline attributes by primary borrower, so this row means the source disagrees with the loan master about who owns the instrument — which affects which zip code the distress is reported under. Carries the event identifier, instrument type and identifier, the event's customer identifier and the resolved primary borrower. Used by the lending data team. Exercised by T23. Filterable by instrument type.
- **`DQ_Customer_Missing_Zip`** — data quality capture. Reads the customer weekly metrics left-joined to the current-primary-address table. One row is a customer with payment activity inside the window who has no current address of type `PRIMARY`, or whose postal code is null or blank. Their payments are counted at customer grain but vanish from every zip rollup, which directly breaks the acceptance criterion that a zip's figures equal the sum of its customers' figures. Carries the customer identifier, name, customer status and the window's payments-due count and amount. Used by the branch operations team, who can correct the address. Exercised by T2. Filterable by customer identifier.
- **`DQ_Address_Missing_State`** — data quality capture. Reads the current-primary-address table. One row is a customer whose current primary address has a null or blank `state_province`, so the zip code that address contributes to can never be found by the dashboard's state search (R9) even though it appears in the unfiltered table. Carries the customer identifier, zip code, city and the address's source update timestamp. Used by the branch operations team. Exercised by T3. Filterable by customer identifier.
- **`DQ_ZipWeekly_Invariant_Violation`** — data quality capture. Reads the zip weekly table and the customer weekly table. One row is a zip-week that violates one of three invariants, named in a `violation` column: percent distressed outside the closed range zero to one hundred; posted on time plus distressed not equal to payments due, by count or by amount; or the zip's counts and amounts not equal to the sum of its customers' rows for that week. This table should always be empty in production; a row means an aggregation defect, not a source defect, and it is the runtime counterpart of the no-rows tests in checklist step 18. Carries the zip code, week start, the violation name and the offending values. Used by the pipeline owner. Filterable by zip code and violation.
- **`OB_SourceIngestionStats`** — observability statistics. Reads the union of the twelve raw source tables, before deduplication and before any filter, projected to a source name and the connector's `ingested_at` column, and tumbles them into one-hour buckets restricted to the run window. One count is the number of records received from that source in that hour. A drop or a missing hour where the source normally delivers means a feed problem; the payment and delinquency feeds are continuous CDC streams, while customer and address feeds are sparse by nature, so empty hours there are normal and must be documented as such in the README. Filterable by source name. If the tumble table-valued function does not compile in batch runtime mode, group on an hour-truncated `ingested_at` instead.
- **`OB_PaymentClassificationStats`** — observability statistics. Reads the classified payment table and the delinquency slot table. One count is the number of rows, and the summed cent amount, for one combination of instrument type, week due and classification, where classification is one of posted on time, posted late, not posted, excluded partial payment, excluded non-posted status, excluded missing posted date, excluded missing due date and excluded unknown instrument. This is the single place that shows how much input each nightly run is dropping and why, and it supplies the per-loan-vehicle breakdown that the requirements' open question 2 asks about without adding a vehicle dimension to the dashboard. Used by the pipeline owner and the credit risk team. Exercised by T15, T16, T17, T19 and T20. Filterable by instrument type and classification.

### Skip

- **Late-arrival and dropped-record counters** — Flink reports records received, records dropped as late and records processed as job metrics. The pipeline also runs in batch mode over bounded sources, where no record is dropped for lateness at all, so a table would always read zero.
- **Total record counts per run** — already covered by Flink job metrics and by `OB_SourceIngestionStats`, which is strictly more informative because it is broken down by source and hour.
- **A capture for payments with no matching delinquency event, or delinquency events with no matching payment** — neither condition is a rule violation. A payment posted on time has no delinquency event by definition, and an unpaid missed payment has no payment by definition; both are the normal path, and the not-posted slot resolution already handles the interesting case.
- **Enum-validity captures for `payment_status`, `payment_type` and `event_type`** — the pipeline classifies every value it recognizes and routes everything else to an explicit exclusion reason that `OB_PaymentClassificationStats` already counts, so an unexpected enum value shows up there as a rising excluded count rather than needing its own table.
- **A capture for customers whose loan status is charged off or paid off** — the pipeline deliberately does not filter on loan or account status, so no record is discarded on that basis and there is nothing to collect.
- **Referential-integrity and enum assertions over the catalog's own test data** — see Reuse below.

### Reuse

- `data-catalog/customer/customer_ontology.sqrl` lines 63–91 already assert, as `/*+test(no_rows) */` build-time checks against the catalog's own test data, that customer identifiers are 6–15 characters (`CustomerHasValidId_Test`, l.65), that `customer_type` and `customer_status` hold only their declared enum values (`CustomerHasValidType_Test` l.69, `CustomerHasValidStatus_Test` l.74), that date of birth is consistent with customer type (`CustomerDOBConsistentWithType_Test` l.79), that every `Customer_Address` row references an existing `Customer` (`AddressReferencesValidCustomer_Test` l.85), and that no customer relationship is self-referential (`RelationshipNotSelfReferential_Test` l.90). These run in the catalog's own test suite, in the read-only sibling module. This project does not duplicate any of them.
- There is **nothing to reuse on the lending side**: `data-catalog/lending/lending-ontology.sqrl` declares relationships only and carries no assertions at all. That absence is the reason the payment and delinquency captures above are built here rather than assumed to exist upstream. Record this gap in the README so the catalog owners can close it; do not add assertions to the read-only module.

## Technical Decisions

**Runtime mode is Flink `BATCH`.** R3 and R4 describe a nightly job that recomputes the whole window and replaces prior results, and R5 requires a customer's entire window history to move to a new zip code on the next run. An incremental streaming aggregate would have to retract every past week of that customer's history on a zip change, which is not expressible; a bounded batch recompute is. The reference for a batch DataSQRL sub-project is the feature-store template's training deployment.

**Enabled engines are flink, postgres and vertx only.** Postgres is enabled because the API serves database-backed point lookups and filtered scans. Vert.x is enabled because the project exposes GraphQL, REST and MCP. Kafka is omitted because the project defines no mutation table and no `SUBSCRIBE` statement — production sources being Kafka topics is a property of the catalog's connectors, not of this project's engine set. Iceberg and any lake query engine are omitted because there is no data-lake output.

**The run anchor is one templated expression in `_RunAnchor`.** The window must be pinned to a fixed date in test or every snapshot rots as the wall clock advances, and must follow the clock in production. A single mustache boolean section on the `pinned_anchor` config key emits a date literal built from `anchor_date` in test and local and `CURRENT_DATE` in prod; batch mode evaluates `CURRENT_DATE` once at query start, so every row of one run shares one anchor. The templated value carries no quote characters, so mustache escaping cannot corrupt it. Putting the expression in exactly one table means the rest of the script never mentions the clock.

**Weeks start on Monday and carry no time zone.** Every date the pipeline groups by — `due_date`, `applied_date`, `posted_date`, `received_date`, `event_date` — is a SQL `DATE` in the source, so there is no instant to convert and the time-zone half of open question 4 does not arise. Monday-start matches ISO weeks and the week start is computed arithmetically from day-of-week rather than from a week-of-year extraction, so it is correct across year boundaries.

**The window is the thirteen most recent complete weeks; the chart is the most recent twelve.** Three months is between twelve and fourteen weeks depending on the anchor, and open question 5 requires that twelve complete weeks are always covered. Thirteen complete weeks guarantees it with a one-week margin and stays inside "the last three months". The current, incomplete week is excluded everywhere, which is what makes the two-week trend comparable.

**`ZipWeekly` is a rollup of `CustomerWeekly`, not an independent aggregation.** The acceptance criterion that a zip's figures equal the sum of its customers' figures then holds by construction rather than by coincidence. Average days late cannot be rolled up by averaging, so `CustomerWeekly` carries the late-posted count and the summed days late as columns and the zip level recomputes the average from those sums.

**Bucket aggregation and lateness aggregation are two separate groupings joined with a full outer join.** The buckets group by week due and the lateness metric groups by week posted; the requirements state explicitly that the two are not comparable. A full outer join on customer and week, with the join keys coalesced, means a week that has a late posting but no payments due still produces a row — which is exactly the case created by a payment due before the window and posted inside it.

**Distress slots are resolved by instrument and due week, never by delinquency `cured_date`.** A missed-payment event becomes a not-posted slot only if no posted-late payment exists for the same instrument whose due date falls in the same week. This is what makes the posted-late and not-posted split shift between runs while the distressed total stays fixed (R6), and it is what guarantees a payment is never counted in both. `cured_date` is not used because a delinquency can be cured by means other than the payment posting, which would silently drop a slot.

**Only `event_type` equal to `MISSED_PAYMENT` produces a not-posted slot.** Every other delinquency event type is a lifecycle record about a missed payment that is already represented, and the mortgage table's `LATE_PAYMENT` value describes a payment that is already in `Mortgage_Payment`. Without this filter one missed payment inflates its week by the number of lifecycle events it generated.

**Lateness is derived from dates, not taken from the source.** `days_past_due_at_payment` and `days_from_due` may embed a source grace period, and the requirements state that no grace periods apply. The derived value drives every bucket and the average; the source value is retained only to feed `DQ_Payment_DaysLate_Mismatch`, which surfaces the disagreement instead of hiding it.

**Attribution is always the instrument's primary borrower, never the delinquency event's own `customer_id`.** The requirements define the customer as the primary borrower for loans with more than one borrower, and the event's customer identifier can name a co-borrower. Deriving from the loan or account record satisfies the criterion that a two-borrower loan counts exactly once, and the disagreement is captured in `DQ_Delinquency_Customer_Mismatch`.

**Loan and account status is deliberately not filtered.** A payment due inside the window on a loan that has since been paid off or charged off still belongs in that week's buckets; filtering by current status would retroactively erase history and break the week-over-week comparison.

**Test data is owned by this project, not taken from the catalog's test environment.** The catalog's lending test data holds seven consumer-loan payments, five credit-card payments and a single delinquency event per dataset, all dated 2023 and 2024. It cannot exercise a thirteen-week window, six zip codes, three states, four instrument types, or any acceptance oracle. Project-owned filesystem connectors declared with `LIKE` against the catalog's schema tables keep the catalog as the single source of schema truth while giving the tests the density they need. The `-prod` file delegates entirely to the catalog's production connectors, so the two environments differ only in where the bytes come from.

**The `script.include` entry for the catalog must set `is_batch` to true.** The catalog's connector scripts branch on that mustache section and the catalog's own package configuration does not define it; an unset section renders falsey, which in production would omit the bounded-scan option and leave the batch job reading the Kafka topics forever.

**The GraphQL schema is inferred, not declared.** The requirements fix no API contract, so declaring a static schema file would add a second place to maintain every column without buying compatibility enforcement. `FULL` endpoint generation over the inferred schema exposes every table and function as a query, a REST endpoint and an MCP tool in one step, which is what R16 and R17 ask for.

**Zip search is two operations, not one overloaded argument.** State filtering is exact equality and is expressed as an optional filter argument on the overview table; prefix filtering needs a pattern match and is expressed as a separate table function with a required argument. Every table function in the DataSQRL templates uses non-null arguments, so a single operation with two optional arguments would rest on an untested nullability behaviour. Two operations also give an MCP agent two unambiguous tools.

**Sorting is client-side and `default-limit` is raised to 1000.** SQRL generates no dynamic sort argument, and the result sets — zip codes matching one filter, customers of one zip code — are small enough to sort in the browser. Raising the default limit means the dashboard receives the full filtered set in one call, so a client-side sort cannot silently reorder only the first page.

**Empty values are null, not zero.** Average days late is null when no late payment was posted that week, percent distressed is null when nothing was due, and a percentage-change trend is null when the prior value is zero or null. Zero would claim a measurement that was never taken; the UI renders null as an em dash. Percent-distressed trend is a difference of percentage points and stays defined whenever both weeks have a value.

**Percent distressed is stored in percentage points, not as a fraction.** The requirements and the acceptance oracles are written in percentage points — 5%, +1%, −3% — so storing 5.00 rather than 0.05 makes the trend column a plain subtraction and removes a rounding decision from the UI.

**`DashboardRun` is a single-row table keyed on a constant.** It carries the anchor and every derived week boundary plus a computed-at timestamp, which answers open question 13 directly: the dashboard shows when the data was last refreshed, and if a nightly run fails, the prior run's row and results remain in Postgres and keep being served. `ZipWeeklyHistory` joins it so that rows written by earlier runs, for weeks that have since left the window, never reach the chart.

**Local is a third environment reusing the test sources.** The local overlay sets the same `environment`, `pinned_anchor` and `anchor_date` values as test but carries no test-runner block, so the pipeline can be run and the UI developed against a served API with deterministic data and without a second connector file.

## Assumptions

- **Customer table time window (open question 1)**: Assumed the drill-down customer table shows the last two complete weeks with trend, mirroring the overview, and a customer appears if they had at least one distressed payment in either of those two weeks. Rationale: the drill-down sits directly under the two-week overview and a different window there would make the two tables silently incomparable. [impact: medium]
- **Loan vehicle breakout (open question 2)**: Assumed no loan-vehicle dimension on the dashboard tables or the chart; the per-vehicle split is available through `OB_PaymentClassificationStats`. Rationale: the requirements never ask for it in any functional requirement, and adding a dimension to every serving table to answer an unconfirmed question would double the API surface. [impact: low]
- **Access and privacy (open question 3)**: Assumed no authentication or authorization in version one; the API is deployed on the internal network, customer-level data is unmasked, and no audit logging is added. Sensitive fields — social security number, tax identification number and date of birth — are never read from the catalog, so they cannot leak. Rationale: the requirement is explicitly unanswered and JWT or OAuth can be added later through the Vert.x engine configuration without changing any table. [impact: medium]
- **Week definition (open question 4)**: Assumed Monday-start weeks, no time-zone conversion because every grouping column is a SQL `DATE`, and the "last two weeks" and the chart both use the most recent *complete* weeks, excluding the week the run falls in. Rationale: an incomplete week always looks less distressed and would corrupt every trend. [impact: high]
- **Window length (open question 5)**: Assumed thirteen complete weeks, with the chart showing the most recent twelve. Rationale: guarantees twelve complete weeks are always covered while staying within "the last three months". [impact: medium]
- **Empty values (open question 6)**: Assumed null for average days late when no late payment was posted, null for percent distressed when nothing was due, and null for a percentage-change trend when the prior value is zero or null; the UI renders null as an em dash. Rationale: zero asserts a measurement that was not taken. [impact: low]
- **Customer information fields (open question 7)**: Assumed customer identifier, first name, last name, customer type, customer status, relationship start date, city, state and zip code. Rationale: enough to find the person in a servicing system without exposing any `@spii` field. [impact: low]
- **Chart layout (open question 8)**: Assumed three vertically aligned charts sharing one week axis — amounts, percent distressed, and average days late — with the third explicitly labelled "by week posted". Rationale: the requirements warn that the layout must not suggest the by-week-posted metric is comparable to the by-week-due metrics, and separate panels say that more clearly than a third axis. [impact: low]
- **Default sort order (open question 9)**: Assumed current-week distressed amount descending, then zip code ascending for the overview and customer identifier ascending for the customer table. Rationale: the user's stated goal is prioritizing intervention, so the most distressed row should already be at the top. [impact: low]
- **State input format (open question 10)**: Assumed two-letter state codes, matched case-insensitively against `state_province` after upper-casing; input whose first character is a digit is treated as a zip prefix, anything else as a state code. Rationale: `state_province` is documented as a state or province code, so a full state name would never match. [impact: low]
- **API and MCP design (open question 11)**: Assumed auto-generated MCP tools named with the `Get` prefix over the inferred schema, and no agent authentication in version one. Rationale: consistent with the no-authentication assumption above, and it keeps the MCP surface identical to the GraphQL surface, which is what R17 requires. [impact: low]
- **Scale (open question 12)**: Assumed at most five million payment rows and five hundred thousand customers inside a thirteen-week window, and at most fifty concurrent dashboard users. Rationale: a mid-size retail bank; batch Flink handles this comfortably on default sizing and the Postgres tables are aggregate-grain, at most a few hundred thousand rows. [impact: low]
- **Job failure and refresh visibility (open question 13)**: Assumed a failed nightly run leaves the previous run's results in Postgres and the dashboard keeps serving them, with the `DashboardRun` refresh date shown in a header banner so the staleness is visible. Rationale: serving yesterday's complete numbers beats serving nothing or a half-written window. [impact: medium]
- **Delinquency event week (Section 4 assumption "to be confirmed")**: Assumed a missed-payment delinquency event's `event_date` falls in the week the missed payment was due, exactly as the requirements state, so `event_date` is the week-due grouping key for not-posted slots. Rationale: the requirements assert it and no field in any delinquency table carries the missed payment's due date. [impact: high]
- **Partial payments (Section 4 assumption "to be confirmed")**: Assumed `payment_type` equal to `PARTIAL` is excluded from every bucket and counted only in `OB_PaymentClassificationStats`. Rationale: the requirements say partial payments are ignored for now, and counting one as a full on-time payment would understate distress. Note that `Credit_Card_Payment.payment_type` has no `PARTIAL` value, so the exclusion applies only to consumer-loan and mortgage payments. [impact: medium]
- **Which payment statuses count as posted**: Assumed `APPLIED` for consumer-loan and mortgage payments and `POSTED` for credit-card payments; `PENDING`, `RETURNED` and `REVERSED` are excluded. Rationale: the enum documentation in the catalog names exactly these as the terminal success values, and a returned payment did not pay anything. [impact: high]
- **Which date counts as the posting date**: Assumed `applied_date` falling back to `received_date` for consumer-loan and mortgage payments, and `posted_date` falling back to `received_date` for credit-card payments. Rationale: the catalog documents `applied_date` as the date the payment was applied and `posted_date` as the date it posted; `received_date` is when it arrived, which is the best available fallback. [impact: high]
- **Zip source is the current `PRIMARY` address only**: Assumed the customer's zip is the `postal_code` of their single current address of type `PRIMARY`, with no fallback to `MAILING` or any other type; customers without one are captured by `DQ_Customer_Missing_Zip` and excluded from zip rollups. Rationale: a mailing address is frequently a post-office box in a different zip and would misplace the distress geographically. [impact: high]
- **Intervention workflows (Section 6, "Please confirm")**: Assumed confirmed as out of scope. The pipeline and API support planning only; no outreach, case-management or write-back surface is built, and no mutation table exists, which is why the Kafka engine is omitted. Rationale: the requirements state the assumption and nothing in R1–R17 implies a write path. [impact: medium]
- **Catalog datasets consumed**: Assumed exactly five catalog scripts are needed — `customer_master`, `consumer_loans`, `credit_cards`, `mortgage_originations` and `mortgage_servicing` — and that `kyc_aml`, `customer_enriched`, `credit_risk_signals`, `mortgage_performance` and the whole `deposits_payments` line of business are not. Rationale: no requirement references risk ratings, household segments, balances or deposit transactions, and every declared dataset is copied into the build folder at compile time. [impact: medium]
- **Include namespace**: Assumed the catalog is included under the namespace `data_catalog` with underscores, pointing at the catalog's `ontology-test-package.json`, rather than the hyphenated namespace the reference template uses. Rationale: a hyphen has a different meaning in SQL and the orchestration guidance names `data_catalog` explicitly. [impact: high]
- **Project artifact naming**: Assumed the main script and the package files are named `distress_dashboard`, not the full directory name. Rationale: shorter and unambiguous; the directory name stays `distressed_loan_dashboard`, and the test-runner script's sub-project name and build folder both use `distress_dashboard`. [impact: low]
- **No large data files and no user-defined functions**: Assumed neither is needed. Every catalog test-data file is under 7 KB and this project's own test data will be of the same order, so nothing triggers large-data handling; and every computation — week-start arithmetic, day differences, conditional aggregation, ratios — is expressible in Flink SQL, so no Java function is required. Rationale: verified by inspecting the catalog test-data file sizes and by checking the temporal function reference for `DAYOFWEEK`, `TIMESTAMPADD` and `TIMESTAMPDIFF`. [impact: low]
